Acre Vision · Hyderabad · project report

Finding vacant land when
nobody has labelled any

A satellite land classifier for a 3,090 km² area of Hyderabad, built without ground truth and then given some. This is the full account: what the first version got wrong, how a 256-parcel verified set was constructed to find out, which upgrades survived testing, which one did not, and where the working system still fails.

Summary

What this is and where it landed

The system scores every 320 m parcel of Hyderabad for how much open, undeveloped land it contains, then feeds that score into a six-factor suitability ranker alongside road, healthcare, education, lifestyle and essential-services access.

The starting point was a model reporting 98.5% accuracy. That number was an artefact: the labels it was graded against were computed from the same spectral indices fed to the model as input, so the model was being asked to reproduce a rule it had already been given. Removing that circularity dropped the honest figure to the sixties. Everything since has been the work of getting it back up legitimately — and of building the measurement apparatus that makes any figure believable at all.

Design

The one rule the whole project rests on

Labels come only from OpenStreetMap vector geometry. The network sees only Sentinel-2 pixels. Neither can see the other's source.

This is the fix for the original failure and it constrains everything downstream. It is why no spectral term may ever enter a labelling rule, however tempting — and why, when spectral indices were later used to audit a label rule, that had to be stated explicitly as a diagnostic rather than a design input.

The model is an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18-U-Net over six channels — R, G, B, NDVI, NDBI, NDWI — reading a 64 px window and scored on the centre 32 px, so each parcel is classified with 160 m of surrounding context it is not judged on. Balanced sampling, D4 augmentation, class-weighted cross-entropy plus Dice, D4 test-time augmentation, three spatially-blocked seeds.

Method

Building ground truth where there was none

At Sentinel-2's 10 m resolution a vacant plot and a low building are a handful of pixels apart, which is exactly why the original labels could never be checked by eye. Verification used Esri World Imagery at zoom 18 — roughly 0.57 m/px, about 300× the detail per unit area — over 256 parcels in three batches.

Two safeguards make the set usable as evidence: the OpenStreetMap mask was withheld during classification, so the labels could not inherit the bias they were meant to measure, and parcels were shuffled, so the sampling stratum could not be inferred from position.

Finding · one

A single tag was poisoning the class

Of twelve verified parcels tagged scrub in OpenStreetMap, ten are dense woody vegetation and two are open land — 17% precision. And scrub was not marginal: at 47.8 of 74.9 km² it dominated the very class it was corrupting. Moving it to the non-developable class lifted the land-use rule from 31% to 75% precision.

Finding · two

Most of the city had no answer key at all

Batch 2 sampled something batch 1 never had: parcels OpenStreetMap assigns no label of any kind. There are 17,921 of them — 60% of the city.

Every accuracy figure up to that point had been computed on the 40% that carried a label. On the rest, no rule could be scored because no rule said anything. Checked against imagery, 22 of the 24 sampled (92%) were genuinely open, developable land — the exact thing the project exists to find.

The cause was a guard added in good faith. To distinguish “empty” from “nobody surveyed this”, the rule required a parcel's 810 m surroundings to be demonstrably mapped before it could be called vacant. That correctly avoids labelling unmapped countryside as vacant — but here the unmapped countryside is vacant, so the guard discarded most of the target class to avoid a risk that barely materialises. Batch 3, drawn only from the land the relaxed rule newly claims, put it at 90% precision (95% CI 82–98%).

Result · rejected

The upgrade that did not survive

Batch 1 showed the remaining errors were settlements OpenStreetMap has not building-mapped. Those places still have their streets mapped, and minor-road density separated them at AUC 0.777. Gating on it lifted Vacant F1 from 80.5 to 83.3.

v7 — rejected

The threshold was fitted on batch 1, so batch 1 could no longer judge it. On batch 2, which it had never seen, it made things slightly worse: Vacant F1 64.9 → 63.9. It had fitted the noise in 46 parcels.

It is reported rather than quietly dropped because it is the reason to trust the rest. The same protocol that promoted v8 rejected v7. On batch 1 alone, v7 would have shipped as a 68→84% precision win.

Review

What manual review of the ranked output found

The vacancy call itself held up: no water bodies surfaced in the top ranks, no obvious false positives, and the amenity subscores behaved as expected. Two other things did come up — and neither is a model error.

Both are cases where the model was asked “is this land open?” and answered correctly, but the question the product needs answered is “can this land be bought and built on?” Nothing in the pipeline has ever asked that. There is no constraint layer.

Floodplain and campus grounds are simultaneously open (high vacancy subscore) and close to amenities (high road and healthcare subscores), which is precisely the combination the composite rewards. A rule that scores openness and proximity, with no notion of tenure, will surface them first.
The fix, and what it costs

An OpenStreetMap-derived developability mask — a 100 m buffer on waterways and a 120 m buffer on institutional POIs — removes 18.1% of parcels and replaces 19 of the unfiltered top 100. It is implemented and switched on by default in the deployed app. It costs nothing but OSM data already in the pipeline, and it is a filter rather than a model change, so it cannot degrade the classifier.

Analysis

Why the parcel is 25 acres, and why that matters here

The parcel size was never a modelling choice. A chip is 32 px, a Sentinel-2 pixel is 10 m, so a parcel is a 320 m square — 10.24 ha, or 25.3 acres. That is simply what one chip covers at this sensor's ground sample distance, and 32 px is about the smallest window from which a convolutional network can read useful spatial context.

Both failure modes above are partly artefacts of that granularity. A 25-acre square laid over a college campus will contain the buildings and the playing fields and whatever vacant plot abuts the boundary wall, and it returns one blended answer. The same square laid across the Musi contains channel, bank and floodplain together. At a finer ground sample distance they stop sharing a chip:

The right answer is probably a cascade: screen the whole city at 10 m, then re-image only the top few hundred parcels at high resolution.
Limits

Other failure modes this configuration should be expected to have

Two of these were measured. The rest are reasoned from the configuration and are flagged as such — they are hypotheses about where it will break, not observations that it has.

The two that are not about satellites at all

The verified set is annotated by one reader, not three. Every number here is measured against 256 parcels classified by a single annotator. pipeline/37_label_gold.py and 38_agreement.py are set up for three-way independent labelling and Fleiss' kappa. Until that runs, the correct reading of every accuracy figure is “agreement with one careful reader”.

“Open” is not “developable”. Zoning, ownership, title and access are not in Sentinel-2 and are not in this pipeline. The developability mask is a first approximation from OpenStreetMap; the real answer needs cadastral data. That gap is the distance between this model and a product.

Next

What to do, in order

Do first Three-way human labelling of the 256 verified parcels

Everything rests on one annotator. Independent agreement between three converts the central claim from evidence into a result. Roughly 40 minutes each; the tool, images and analysis script are all in the repository.

Do first Run the model on a monsoon composite

The largest untested risk. Score an August scene with the same weights and compare the ranking to this one. If the top 100 substantially reorders, “vacant” is a seasonal reading rather than a property of the land, and the whole framing needs adjusting. One afternoon of work.

Then Extend the developability mask

Waterway and institutional buffers are in. Add flood-zone polygons, cantonment boundaries and, where obtainable, cadastral ownership. This is where the model becomes a product.

Then A high-resolution second stage

Screen the AOI at 10 m as now, then re-image only the top few hundred parcels at sub-metre resolution to separate plot from campus and channel from bank. Cheaper than sweeping 3,090 km² commercially, and it resolves both measured failure modes.

Then Audit the five amenity subscores

They were inherited unexamined. education and essential_services average 2.2/100 — nearly every parcel scores zero, so two of the six sliders barely move the ranking at all.
